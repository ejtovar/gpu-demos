Supersonic

Compressible Euler equations (ideal gas, γ = 1.4) solved on your GPU with a WebGPU port of ryujin: continuous Q1 finite elements, graph viscosity with a guaranteed maximum wave speed, an entropy-viscosity indicator, convex limiting of density and specific entropy, and third-order explicit Runge–Kutta. Single precision.

The setup is ryujin's Mach 3 cylinder benchmark: a channel [0, 4] × [−1, 1], a cylinder of diameter 0.5, supersonic inflow on the left, outflow on the right, slip walls. Here the body is cut out of a uniform grid instead of using a body-fitted mesh, and you can move it, rotate it, or swap it for a square, a wedge, or an airfoil (NACA 4-digit profiles use ryujin's airfoil formulas). You can also draw obstacles, read lift and drag, and let the gas forces fly the body.

Lift and drag come from the pressure on the body, F = Σ pi νi with νi the discrete boundary normal. Parts thinner than about two grid cells (sharp edges, thin lines) are trimmed, which on the coarse grids costs thin airfoils some lift; on a double wedge at 5° and Mach 3 the 513 × 257 grid is within about 10% of shock-expansion theory.

Verification: on ryujin's isentropic-vortex test (33 × 33 nodes, t = 2) the error norms agree with ryujin's double-precision reference output to within 3·10⁻⁴ (relative).
